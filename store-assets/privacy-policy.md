# Privacy policy for Deja

**Last updated: 25 October 2026**

Deja does not collect, transmit or share any personal data. There is no account, no
analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no server to send anything to.

This is not a promise that rests on good intentions. **Deja declares no internet
permission**, and explicitly removes the network permissions its libraries would
otherwise have added. An app without that permission is prevented by Android itself from
opening a network connection. You can check this on the Play Store listing ("About this
app" → "App permissions") and in your phone's Settings.

## What Deja reads

Deja reads the screenshots on your phone — the Screenshots folder, and nothing else in your
photo library — so you can search them by what they say. If you share an image into Deja
from another app, Deja reads that image too.

All reading happens on your device, using text-recognition and barcode models that ship
inside the app. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing is downloaded: Deja works the same in
aeroplane mode.

## What Deja stores, and where

Everything Deja keeps is stored on your device, in the app's own private storage, which no
other app can read.

- **The index** — the text read from each screenshot, where on the screen each line was,
  what Deja found in it (for example an amount, a booking reference or a one-time code), the
  category it was sorted into, a small fingerprint used to spot duplicates, and whether you
  starred it to keep.
- **Search history** — the last eight searches that found something.
- **Settings** — your theme choice, whether new-screenshot notifications are on, and
  bookkeeping about the last scan.
- **A crash log** — if Deja crashes, the technical error report (no screenshot content) is
  kept on the phone so you can choose to send it to us. It is never sent automatically.

Images you share into Deja are saved to the `Pictures/Deja` folder on your phone so they
stay available after the app you shared from closes. They are ordinary photos in your
gallery: you can delete them there, or from Deja's Clean up screen.

Sensitive values — one-time codes, Wi-Fi passwords, card, Aadhaar, PAN, passport and other
ID numbers — are kept out of the search index and are masked on screen until you tap them.

## Deleting it

**Privacy → "Delete everything Deja knows"** wipes the index, the search history and the
crash log in one step. Your screenshots themselves are not touched. Uninstalling Deja also
deletes everything it stored.

Android's automatic backup is switched off for Deja, so the index is not copied to your
Google account.

## Exporting it

**Privacy → "Export what Deja knows"** writes the whole index to a file at a location you
choose. Sensitive values are left out of the export unless you choose to include them. That
file is yours; if you save it to a folder a cloud service syncs, that service's own privacy
policy applies to its copy.

## Permissions, and why

- **Photos and videos (images only)** — to read your screenshots. On Android 14 and later
  you can allow only selected photos instead; Deja then reads just those, and lets you add
  more at any time.
- **Notifications** — only if you switch on "Tell me what's in a new screenshot" in the
  Privacy screen. It is off until you turn it on. The notification is created on your
  device; nothing is received from anywhere.
- **Run in the background / after restart** — so a scan of a large library can finish
  after you leave the app, and so the new-screenshot notification keeps working after a
  reboot. These grant no access to anything outside your phone.

Deja has no internet permission.

## Contacting us

If you write to us — from "Get help" or "Report a problem" in the app — your own email or
WhatsApp app sends the message, and you see exactly what is in it before it goes. We use it
only to answer you.

## Children

Deja does not collect data from anyone, including children.
